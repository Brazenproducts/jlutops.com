The Jeep Wrangler JLU offers something no other mainstream SUV can match: genuine roof versatility. You can drive it completely closed, fully open, or any combination in between. But that freedom comes with real trade-offs — and those trade-offs look completely different depending on your climate, how you use the vehicle, and whether you're willing to wrestle a 150-pound hardtop twice a year.

This guide covers every top option for the JLU platform honestly. We're not going to tell you one system is universally superior. We're going to help you figure out which one fits your actual life.

The Factory Hardtop: Insulation, Noise, and the Seasonal Struggle

The factory JLU hardtop is a three-piece unit: two removable front "freedom panels" and a large fixed rear section. For daily driving in four-season climates, it's genuinely excellent. Wind noise is dramatically lower than any soft top. Insulation keeps the cabin warmer in winter and cooler in summer. Break-in resistance is real compared to any canvas alternative.

The JLU hardtop is substantially heavier than the 2-door JL top — expect 140–160 lbs for the full rear section alone. Solo removal is not realistic without a hoist. Many owners buy a hardtop storage cart or overhead hoist system specifically for this twice-a-year transition. Budget $200–$400 for a quality hoist setup if you plan to go topless seasonally.

Freedom Panels: The Compromise Position

If full top removal is too much trouble, the factory hardtop's two front freedom panels give you partial open-air experience. The panels are manageable at around 20 lbs each and store in the cargo area. Remove them and you have a T-bar open-air feel — quieter than a full open Jeep, warmer in shoulder seasons, and far less commitment than a full top removal.

Many JLU hardtop owners never remove the full top — they run freedom panels in spring, summer, and fall, and keep the full top sealed during winter. This is a completely valid approach that avoids the storage challenge entirely.

Premium Soft Tops: Bestop, Smittybilt, and Beyond

Soft tops trade insulation and quiet for weight, easy installation, and lower cost. The JLU soft top market is dominated by a handful of quality players, each with distinct strengths.

Bestop Trektop NX — Community Consensus Champion

The Bestop Trektop NX is the overwhelming community favorite for JLU soft tops. It's not the cheapest option, but it wins on build quality, wind noise management, and long-term durability. The vinyl weight and window clarity exceed budget alternatives significantly. It configures easily between full closed, sunroof-style open, and bikini positions. Retail runs $900–$1,200 for the JLU 4-door version.

Important: Make sure you're ordering the JLU (4-door) version. The 2-door and 4-door Bestop Trektop NX are different products with different frame configurations.

Smittybilt Premium Soft Top — Best Value

If the Bestop price is hard to justify, Smittybilt's premium soft top delivers 80% of the performance at 60% of the cost. Wind noise is slightly higher, the vinyl is marginally thinner, and installation takes a bit more patience. For owners who run topless most of the time and only want a top for wet weather, Smittybilt makes practical sense.

Rugged Ridge All-Terrain Soft Top

Rugged Ridge occupies the middle tier: better than Smittybilt's base models, not quite at Bestop's level. Their All-Terrain soft top uses a dual-panel design that stores the rear passenger panels without tools — a genuine convenience win over competitors.

The Mopar Sky One-Touch: Power Convenience at a Premium

The Mopar Sky One-Touch is a factory-engineered power-folding soft top that retracts with a button press, much like a convertible. It's available as an OEM option on certain JLU builds and as an aftermarket dealer-installed accessory.

The appeal is obvious: effortless open-air experience with zero struggle. Push a button, and the top folds neatly into a storage position behind the rear seats. Push it again and it deploys fully in under 30 seconds.

The trade-offs: Cost ($2,500–$3,500 installed), added complexity (motorized systems can fail), and the fact that it still performs like a soft top — more wind noise than a hardtop, less insulation, more weather vulnerability at the seams.

For owners who primarily want open-air convenience and can justify the premium, the Sky One-Touch is a genuine quality-of-life upgrade. For trail-focused owners who remove the top completely anyway, it's harder to justify over a standard soft top.

Modular Hardtop Systems: The Best of Both Worlds?

Modular hardtops attempt to combine hardtop insulation with soft top flexibility by breaking the structure into manageable panels. The factory Mopar 3-piece Freedom Top is the most common: the two front panels remove independently while the rear hardtop section stays in place.

Third-party modular systems take this further. Some replace the factory hardtop with an entirely new panel system that stores more conveniently or offers different configurations. Brands like Softopper have even created hybrid systems that mount over existing hard tops.

Who Benefits Most from Modular Systems?

  • Owners who want partial open-air driving without full top removal
  • Those in climates where the rear section should stay covered but front ventilation is desirable
  • Owners who garage-keep their JLU and want seasonal versatility without buying two separate top systems
  • Anyone transporting rear passengers who want overhead weather protection while the driver gets open air

Bikini Tops and Sunshades: Minimal Coverage for Maximum Sun

Bikini tops are the bare minimum — they shade the front occupants while leaving the rear completely open. They're summer accessories that get used between full soft top and completely topless operation. Bestop, Pavement Ends, and RT Off-Road all make quality bikini tops for the JLU.

Pair a bikini top with a rear windshield sunshade or rear cargo cover for a practical summer configuration that protects gear from direct sun without closing up the cabin.

Storage Solutions: The Overlooked Part of Top Ownership

One thing nobody tells you before buying a JLU: where you store the top when it's off is as important as which top you choose. The JLU hardtop rear section is large and heavy. Without a proper storage solution, it ends up leaning against a garage wall — where it gets scratched, knocked over, or damaged.

Recommended storage approaches:

  • Hardtop hoist kit — Ceiling-mounted pulley system raises the top out of the way. $150–$350. Requires garage ceiling clearance.
  • Hardtop cart/dolly — Rolling padded cart stores the top at waist height and doubles as a work surface. $200–$400.
  • Wall-mounted bracket — Stores the top vertically on a padded wall bracket. Space-efficient for tight garages.
  • Soft top storage bag — For soft tops, a weatherproof storage bag protects the folded top when stored between seasons.

Frequently Asked Questions About JLU Tops

What tops are available for the Jeep Wrangler JLU?

The JLU supports a factory hardtop, Mopar Sky One-Touch power soft top, traditional soft tops (Bestop, Smittybilt, etc.), modular hardtops, and various freedom panel configurations. Third-party options expand this considerably.

Is the JLU hardtop worth keeping?

For climates with cold winters and rain, yes absolutely. The JLU hardtop offers the best insulation, quietest cabin, and highest security. The main downside is weight — JLU hardtops weigh 140–160 lbs and require help or a hoist to remove seasonally.

What is the best soft top for a JLU?

The Bestop Trektop NX is widely considered the best JLU soft top for most owners. It offers easy operation, high-quality vinyl, multiple configurations, and excellent wind noise management compared to cheaper alternatives.

How do I remove the JLU hardtop by myself?

Solo hardtop removal requires a hoist or lift system. Options include overhead hoist kits ($200–$400), dedicated hardtop carts, or pneumatic lift systems. The JLU top weighs significantly more than the 2-door JL top due to the extended rear section.

What is the Mopar Sky One-Touch top?

The Mopar Sky One-Touch is a power-operated soft top that folds back with a button press. It offers sunroof-style open-air freedom without full removal. It's a factory option on some trims and available as an aftermarket add-on.

Can I use a 2-door JL top on my JLU?

No. The JLU uses a longer top to accommodate the 4-door configuration and extended wheelbase. JL 2-door and JLU tops are not interchangeable.

Do soft tops reduce wind noise compared to a hardtop?

No — soft tops generate more wind noise than a well-sealed hardtop at highway speeds. However, premium soft tops like the Bestop Trektop NX are significantly quieter than budget alternatives and acceptable for most highway driving.

What is a modular hardtop for the JLU?

Modular hardtops break down into individual panels for partial removal. Options like the Mopar 3-piece hardtop allow you to remove the front panels while keeping the rear section intact — giving open-air feel with rear passenger weather protection.

How much does a replacement JLU soft top cost?

Entry-level JLU soft tops start around $350–$450 (Smittybilt, Rugged Ridge). Mid-range options like the Bestop Trektop NX run $900–$1,200. The Mopar Sky One-Touch power top is $2,500–$3,500 installed.

Which JLU top is best for four-season use?

The factory hardtop or an aftermarket modular hardtop is best for year-round use. Hardtops provide genuine insulation, weather sealing, and security that no soft top can match in harsh winters or sustained rain.
